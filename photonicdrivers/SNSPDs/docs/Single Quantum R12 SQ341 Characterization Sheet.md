Appendix 1: Characterization methods
Dark count rate
The dark count rate (DCR) was measured by sweeping the bias current of the detectors with a step size of 0.1 µA and an
integration time of 60 s. During this measurement, all the fiber ports in the front panel of the system were closed, and
environmental light was minimized. The reported DCR value for a detector is at a fixed biasing current which is defined
as the operating current.
Efficiency
The efficiency experiments were done by coupling a laser light source to the system with wavelengths of either 1309 nm
or 1545 nm. The input power towards the system was 3.0 nW + 50 dB attenuation or 2.0 nW + 50 dB attenuation for the
1309 nm or 1545 nm light source, respectively. This can be calculated as an input photon flux of 198k or 156k photons
per second, respectively.
With the light source coupled to the system, the bias current of the detectors was swept with a step size of 0.1 µA and
an integration time of 600 ms. The trigger level in the driver interface is typically set at 100 – 200 mV.

Timing Jitter
The jitter experiments were done by a pulsed laser light source (Fianium FM-1060) with a built-in photodiode, and an
oscilloscope (Lecroy, 4 GHz bandwidth and 40 GS/s sampling). Light pulses of <5 ps were generated with a repetition rate
of 50 MHz. The laser light was fiber-coupled to the cryostat and the optical input power was attenuated until a detector
count rate of 100.000 cps was reached. The electrical output of both the built-in photodiode of the pulsed light source
and the SNSPD detector were coupled to the individual channels of the oscilloscope.
The oscilloscope was set to measure the difference in arrival time between the electrical output pulses of the built-in
photodiode and the detector, and store the data in a histogram over 100.000 detection events. The trigger levels were
set to the steepest part of the rising edge of both the output pulses.
The timing jitter was determined by the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of a Gaussian curve fitted over the histogram.
The jitter measurements were done at the operating current of each detector, unless mentioned otherwise.
Pulse amplitude
The pulse amplitude was measured by coupling the electrical output of the SNSPD detector to an oscilloscope (Lecroy, 4
GHz bandwidth and 40 GS/s sampling rate). The pulse amplitude was then determined by the maximum value of the 100
times averaged pulse. The reported pulse amplitude value for a detector is at a fixed operating current.
