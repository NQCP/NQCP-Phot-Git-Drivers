Appendix 1: Characterization methods
Efficiency
The efficiency curve is generated by coupling either a 785 nm or 943 nm laser source at 10 nW, or a 1310 nm or 1550 nm
laser source at 1.5 nW, which will be attenuated with 50 dB. This can be calculated as 395k, 475k, 99k or 117k photons
coming in every second, respectively. With the photon source attached we sweep the bias current over the detectors
with a step size of 0.1 µA and an integration time of 200 ms.
With the known input photon flux, the system detection efficiency (SDE) can be calculated by: measured count rate /
input photon flux x100%
Dark count rate
The dark count rate is measured by sweeping the bias current over the detectors at 0.1 µA intervals, with an integration
time of 60 s. During this measurement, no optical fiber is attached to the system.
Timing Jitter
The jitter is measured with a fast pulse laser with a built-in fast photodiode (Fianium FM-1060) and a high-end
oscilloscope. Laser pulses of <5 ps are generated with a repetition rate of 50 MHz. This signal is optically coupled to the
SNSPD. The output of both the built-in photodiode and the SNSPD are coupled into the oscilloscope. The oscilloscope is
set to trigger at the steepest part of the detection pulse of the SNSPD.
The oscilloscope measures the time difference between the electrical output of the laser source and the electrical output
of the SNSPD and stores the measured data in a histogram.
From this distribution, the jitter can be calculated by measuring the full width at half maximum (FWHM).
Efficiency at high count rates
With increasing input photon flux the chance of a photon not being detected due to it arriving at the SNSPD within the
recovery time of the SNSPD increases, decreasing detection efficiency. We therefore measure the SDE at increasing
count rates. We use a pulsed and CW laser at a fixed repetition rate for the pulsed laser and increase the photon flux in
steps. We use this to measure the detection efficiency as a function of detector count-rate.
